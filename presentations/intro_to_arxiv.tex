\begin{frame}{arXiv}
	What is it?
	\begin{itemize}
		\item A server that hosts preprints (versions of papers prior to publication) in physics, math, statistics, computer science
		\item There are other preprint servers (e.g., medRxiv) but this is the one I mostly use
	\end{itemize}
	~\\
	Why use arXiv?
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Make preliminary research freely available and accessible
		\item Opportunity for wider feedback from peers or technical details for presentations
		\item `Stake your claim' on a topic while going through peer-review
	\end{itemize}
	~\\
	A quick overview of arXiv
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Submitting to arXiv}
	The first time you submit, you will need someone to \textit{endorse} you
	\begin{itemize}
		\item This is to prevent spam from being posted
	\end{itemize}
	~\\
	I may be able to endorse you, but I also have other contacts
	~\\~\\
	In general, prefers use of \texttt{.tex} files, but can also upload a PDF of a Word doc
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Considerations}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Have you discussed with co-authors?
		\item Have you checked the preprint policies of the journals you may submit to?\footnote[frame]{Most are fine, but some (e.g., \textit{JAMA}) have historically had restrictions}
		\item Is arXiv the right fit? Or is there a better preprint server for your topic?
	\end{itemize}
	~\\
	In general, I support the use of preprint servers. I think they are particularly valuable for receiving pre-publication feedback and offer a record of how a work changes through peer-review
\end{frame}
