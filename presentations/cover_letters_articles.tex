\begin{frame}{Cover Letters}
	Most journals require submission of a cover letter
	\begin{itemize}
		\item This is an opportunity to highlight aspects of your paper in a more casual way
		\item Explain why your paper is relevant to that journal
		\item Upon resubmissions communicate information not shared with reviewers
	\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Format}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Find letterhead for your institution
		\item Address letter to that journal specifically and editor
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Initial submission: look up editor(s)-in-chief
			\item Resubmission: managining editor
		\end{itemize}
		\item Have you title and signature for sign-off
	\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{One Recipe for Writing}
	The following is the meta structure I follow. It has worked well for me, but others might use different structures
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Paragraph 1:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Preamble. Introduce title of paper, state journal and article type, any author contribution statement as required by journal
		\end{itemize}
		\item Paragraph 2:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Highlight gap(s) in prior research, how your paper addresses them
		\end{itemize}
		\item Paragraph 3:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item State why your paper is of interest to readership of that journal. Provide 3-5 specific points about why readers will care
			\item Helpful to review aims of journal online
		\end{itemize}
	\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Paragraph 1}
	Example
	\begin{itemize}
		\item``We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled `\textit{[paper title]}' as a \textit{[article type]} for exclusive consideration in \textit{[Journal]}. All authors contributed to and approve of its submission \textit{[or description of who contributed to what aspect]}"
	\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Paragraph 2}
	Example
	\begin{itemize}
		\item ``A fundamental challenge in \textit{[topic area]} is to \textit{[problem]}. Prior research has \textit{[concise summary based on introduction]}. To address this challenge, we \textit{[how you address the problem introduced]}. 
	\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Paragraph 3}
	Example:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item ``Our work will be of intersted to the readership of \textit{[Journal]}. First, our manuscript \textit{[summarize how problem is addressed, why their readership cares about that problem]}. Next, we \textit{[strength of your paper]}. Finally, we \textit{[connection to aims of journal]}. Thank you for your consideration of our work."
	\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
