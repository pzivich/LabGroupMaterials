# Research Project Checklist

## Instructions

The following is a segmented checklist for research projects. The **purpose** behind this checklist is to help ensure 
the readiness, efficiency, and validity of a research project. This checklist was inspired by pre-flight checklists used
by pilots for air travel. 

This checklist is split into three parts that are meant to be completed at different stages: pre-project, 
pre-submission, and post-acceptance. The pre-project phase corresponds to the development of your research idea. It 
is the 'table setting' or scoping part of your project that allows you to develop a successful paper. The pre-submission
phase occurs prior to your submission of the manuscript to an academic journal (or other relevant submission). The 
post-acceptance phase is what to do after your paper has been accepted. Ultimately, this checklist is for your personal 
reference (and you can add further steps to it), but these are all questions you should be ready to answer at each 
corresponding phase if I were to ask you about it.

**How to use**: create a copy of this file in a folder that corresponds to the specific project. As you progress 
through the stages of your project, complete and verify the relevant parts. Some elements of the checklist have space
for you to provide additional details about how that criteria was met. These should be filled in. Finally, the steps 
are presented in a general recommended ordering (not strict, but are logically ordered).

## Pre-Submission

This phase should be verified prior to the initial submission to a journal. Use this checklist after all authors have
approved of the submission to a journal.

- [ ] All co-authors have read and approved the final version
- [ ] Conflicts of interest and funding sources for all authors are disclosed accurately
- [ ] Completed any relevant checklists for the project (e.g., STROBE, PRISMA, TRIPOD)
- [ ] Verified manuscript adheres to journal's requirements (e.g., word count, structured abstract)
- [ ] Cover letter drafted, states novelty/significance clearly

## Post-Acceptance

Congrats! If you made it to this step, it means your paper has been accepted. Taking an idea from its conception to a 
publication can be quite a process, so don't forget to celebrate. There are a few remaining steps to go that are still 
important steps in the scientific publication process detailed here. 

- [ ] Notify collaborators of publication acceptance and send formatted reference for the paper
- [ ] Celebrate!
- [ ] Copyright or license agreement completed
- [ ] Initiate the NIHMS submission process (if not done by the journal)
- [ ] Verify the article proofs, with additional verification by second or senior author
- [ ] If posted a preprint, update with the published DOI as indicated
- [ ] Update CV to include publication
- [ ] Archive associated files for long-term storage and reference
